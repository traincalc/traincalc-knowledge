# TrainCalc Strategy

## Business goal

Grow sustainable revenue by increasing qualified traffic to calculator pages while preserving healthy product engagement.

## Current strategic thesis

TrainCalc is primarily a calculator-driven acquisition product, not a generic health-content website. Its strongest near-term opportunity is calories and energy expenditure, narrow calculator intent, proven existing pages, calculator clusters with internal-linking value, and Bing opportunities that do not require high domain authority.

## Main growth vectors

- Improve proven calculator pages, beginning with Bing opportunities and protecting Google performance.
- Expand selectively into tightly related calculator intent only after current priorities are complete or consciously reprioritized.
- Validate Pinterest as a measured distribution channel before scaling it.
- Build authority only where it supports acquisition; authority is not the primary KPI.

## Execution order

1. Finish and measure active page-level work.
2. Select the next existing-page opportunity using verified page and query data.
3. Validate bounded distribution experiments.
4. Approve new calculators only with evidence and a clear cluster fit.

## Decision principles

- Favor evidence, query intent, and revenue potential over broad content volume.
- Treat Google and Bing as separate channels when evaluating changes.
- Prefer reversible, focused interventions over broad rewrites.
- Keep the knowledge base manual and lightweight until repeated use demonstrates a need to change it.

## Constraints

- Limited development time.
- Low domain authority (approximately DR 2).
- Google visibility is unstable.
- Current metrics need a verified reporting snapshot before precise trend claims.

## Strategy revisit triggers

- Four manual weekly reviews are complete.
- A material, verified change in channel mix, revenue, or engagement occurs.
- The Calorie Deficit or Pinterest experiment produces a conclusive result.
- A new calculator candidate has evidence strong enough to compete with active priorities.
